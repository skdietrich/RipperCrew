01 / Open the file

The city
that bleeds.

Chicago, 1981. A red van moves through a city already accustomed to looking away.

Chicago Ripper Crew: Reboot follows the crimes of Robin Gecht, Edward Spreitzer, Andrew Kokoraleis, and Thomas Kokoraleis through the people they attacked, the investigation that closed around them, and the imagined perspective of Detective Sarah Novak.

This companion opens the book from another direction. Follow a chapter, examine the legal outcomes, return to a name, or watch material from the original site’s video collection.

How to read this archive

The novel combines historical events with invented dialogue, interior lives, and reconstructed scenes. Sarah and Jenny Novak are fictional. Entries identify the book’s narrative inventions; linked court opinions provide a route into the historical record.

Edition notes / laboratory work

1982 had its own limits.

The revised manuscript describes blood groups, secretor status, enzyme markers, fibers, and physical evidence. It does not present the original investigation as a modern DNA identification exercise.

Gecht’s later requests for DNA testing are discussed in the 2008 appellate opinion. Those post-conviction requests are a different matter from the methods available during the original investigation.

Read the appellate opinion ↗
Edition notes / identification

A vehicle someone remembered.

The red Dodge is a link between the survivor’s account and the investigation. The revised glossary describes blue-and-white feathers at the mirror and a wooden partition inside the van.

The book’s partial number plate beginning with “7” is fictionalized. The October 20 stop by Thomas Flynn appears in the Spreitzer appellate record.

Read the account of the stop ↗
Narrative reconstruction / fictional character

The detective’s burden.

Sarah Novak’s grief for her sister Jenny gives the novel its investigative point of view. Their histories, Sarah’s private thoughts, and her continuing cold-case work are fiction.

The real investigation involved Chicago and suburban detectives. The companion keeps Sarah’s narrative alongside that record without attributing her actions to an actual officer.

08 / Sources & narrative method

The record.
The reconstruction.

The revised third edition is the basis for this companion’s chapter notes, glossary details, and narrative distinctions. It describes its use of fictional scenes, dialogue, symbolism, and personal histories.

The opinions linked here are primary legal texts hosted by Justia. They document particular proceedings and evidence discussed by the courts; they are not a substitute for the entire investigative file.

A date that differs between accounts

The revised manuscript uses October 6, 1982, for Beverly Washington’s attack. The background section of People v. Gecht (2008) gives October 5. The companion identifies the discrepancy rather than silently treating the two dates as identical.
